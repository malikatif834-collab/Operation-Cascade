Five Eyes Advisory  ·  Issued May 2026

Agentic AI systems are currently operating across critical infrastructure. The risks are real. The immaturity of relevant standards is actively amplifying them.

A coordinated advisory from the Canada Cyber Centre, CISA, NSA, the United Kingdom, Australia, and New Zealand establishes the current threat as present fact — not projection. Two immediate requirements follow.

  • Expand threat intelligence through cross-sector collaboration.
  • Develop robust, agent-specific evaluations. Neither requirement has been met.

Which regulatory body has standing?

When harm propagates through a fourth-tier supplier across four jurisdictions, no existing framework identifies which regulatory body has standing to act. Bill C-8 mandates fines up to $15M and personal director liability — but only against the operator at the downstream end, who did not direct the agent.

Which framework governs liability?

Under Canada’s federal privacy framework, transferring data processing to a third party does not transfer accountability. Organizations remain legally responsible for their entire supply chain. But no framework maps that responsibility onto multi-tier autonomous agent pipelines.

Who holds the duty of care when no human intent existed?

NIST’s critical infrastructure concept note and the EU AI Act signal urgency. Neither produces an enforceable standard for the precise interaction — agent to agent, across organizational lines, without a human in the loop — that this threat exploits.

Operation Cascade  ·  The Response

O

peration Cascade is the mechanism built to bridge the Five Eyes guidance and the standards that do not yet exist. It is a G7-level agentic supply-chain threat simulation that generates the empirical foundation necessary for a living, accredited national standard — through three interlocking pillars that the exercise is designed to produce simultaneously.

01

Reproducible Methodology

Scalable controlled adversarial scenarios, built on existing university research infrastructure and cloud environments. Runs scenarios that can be replicated and expanded as the threat landscape evolves.

02

Empirical Field Data

Not modeled risk — documented behavior. Technical propagation, human governance decisions, framework breakdowns, and jurisdictional friction captured during a live multi-sector exercise. The record that a standard requires.

03

A Living Standard

A legally defensible accredited national standard for Canada, built entirely on field-validated data. Harmonized through the Digital Governance Standards Institute, ISO, and the OECD to serve as the G7 reference model.

Cascading impact of an accredited standard

Procurement Authority

Government agencies specify compliance as a contract condition. The standard becomes the market floor, enforced through every public sector RFP across G7 member states.

Regulatory Oversight

Regulators across sectors adopt the standard by reference, building technical requirements into their frameworks. Documented compliance becomes legal evidence of due diligence under Bill C-8 and equivalent frameworks.

International Harmonization

Through Canada’s standards infrastructure — DGSI, ISO, and the OECD — the exercise produces the international reference model that allied democratic economies need and do not yet have.

The coalition assembled

  • National cybersecurity bodies — mandate to protect infrastructure
  • Government standards agencies — authority to set digital compliance metrics
  • Critical-infrastructure operators — managing the active supply chains
  • Top-tier academic researchers — intersection of technical execution and legal theory
  • International research partners — ensuring borderless relevance and harmonization
  • Provincial and municipal authorities — downstream nodes in the cascade

No further authorization is required to begin.

T

he institutional authority required to execute this mandate is already assembled. Participation in Operation Cascade is not open enrollment — it is reserved for institutions with operational standing in the sectors the exercise models. The Evaluation Committee considers each application against the three questions that follow.

  1. i

    Who are you?

    Your institution, your mandate, your stake in the problem. What sector do you operate in, and what is your operational relationship to agentic supply chains or the governance of critical infrastructure?

  2. ii

    What do you bring?

    Operational data. Research capacity. Simulation expertise. Governance authority. Procurement influence. What does your participation make possible that is otherwise impossible — and what does your absence leave unrepresented?

  3. iii

    Why does this matter to you?

    Not a credentials summary. Tell us what is at stake for your organization — and why you need to be in the room when this exercise runs and the standard is written.

Applications are reviewed by the Operation Cascade Evaluation Committee.
Selected participants are contacted directly. There is no waitlist.
